2  Municipal Overview

2.1 Progress to Date

The Town of Amherst Municipality was responsible for 4487.6 Metric Tons of CO2 Equivalent emissions in FY 2016. In FY 2025, this number was 3,732. This represents a 16.8% decrease in municipal emissions relative to the 2016 baseline, which failed to meet the Town’s 25% emissions reduction goal for 2025. To meet the 2030 goal, the Town would need to reach 2,244 MTCO2e emissions, which represents a near 40% decrease in current emissions over the span of 5 years.

As a result, totals shown in the following figures may differ slightly from the headline inventory totals.

2.2 Emissions by Sector

Municipal emissions fall into two categories: stationary energy and transportation. Stationary energy accounted for 73.9% of total emissions in 2025, up from 68.6% in 2022. As it has been reported, electricity usage from electric vehicles (EVs) has been absorbed into stationary energy, so this increase in electricity consumption may reflect the increased adoption of EVs and their miscategorization as stationary energy, rather than a simple reduction in transportation emissions.

2.3 Emissions by Fuel Type

As shown in Figure 2.3, the composition of fuel usage has changed over time within the municipality. Most notably, electricity has seen an increased share of total emissions, from 36% in 2022 to 46% in 2025, while the relative shares of gasoline and diesel have decreased. This is consistent with the Town’s move toward more renewable energy sources.
